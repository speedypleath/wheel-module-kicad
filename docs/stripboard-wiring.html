Stripboard Wiring Guide

Bench reference for the encoder sensor board on continuous-strip Veroboard. Every net, cut and column below is read back out of the built .kicad_pcb, not from intent — if this page and the board disagree, the board is right.

Read this first: a strip is shared by every hole in its row. On plain perfboard a hole connects to nothing. Here it connects to the whole row, so a pin you never wired still shorts to whatever else sits on its strip. The question is never “did I connect the right pads” but “does anything else sit on this strip”.

Row 10 must be cut at column 12. Pico pin 8 (GND) is on the left of that row and pin 33 (AGND) on the right. AGND is a deliberate no-connect; tying it to GND recreates a power_out/power_out ERC violation and breaks a design invariant.

Rows 5, 15 and 20 are deliberately NOT cut. They carry GND on both sides of the Pico (pins 3/38, 13/28, 18/23), so leaving them whole buys six GND connections and the GND bus for free. Getting this backwards costs either a short or a dozen needless wires.

Board map

Component side, Pico USB at the top — matching renders/stripboard-top.png. Strips are drawn in the colour of the net they carry; faint bands are strips with only an unused pin on them, or bare spare board. The copper is on the back, so when you flip the board to cut, columns mirror. Hover any strip for its net and the pads on it; use the chips to isolate one net class.

× = cut

The circuit sits in the top-left, cols 1–28 and rows 1–29. Everything beyond that is spare board — but not inert: each strip still runs the full 40 columns, so the right-hand end of a row is electrically the same node as the part the circuit uses. Free tie points, and somewhere to keep clear of stray bridges.

Three parts are slightly off-grid, and that is fine — the leads bend. J1 is a 2.50 mm connector on a 2.54 mm grid, so pin 6 lands 0.2 mm above row 10, cumulative over six pins; its pins are 0.6 mm in 1.0 mm holes. C1 (2.50 mm) and C2 (5.00 mm) are 0.04 mm and 0.08 mm out.

R4 and R5 are the nicest part of this layout. They reach straight from the Pico's /ENC_A and /ENC_B pins down to the _RAW rows with no link wire at all, so the two noise-sensitive nets are pure copper.

Link wires — 8 of them

Insulated wire on the solder side, hole to hole. The component side is crowded — the Pico spans twenty rows and J1, J2 and the LEDs sit on the very edges the long links follow — so wires there would lie across component bodies. The back is flat: insulated wire over a copper strip touches nothing. Colours follow the console convention, so a net is the same colour on every board in the build: red power, blue GND (the kit has no black), yellow the I²C/IRQ bus, green /LNK.

Wire 7 is one length soldered at five holes — rows 5, 10, 15, 20 and 27. Those three intermediate joints are what tie the Pico's GND rows into one bus; skip any of them and that row floats. The board file models them as three extra zero-length links, which is why the netlist counts twelve links and this table nine wires. They show on the map as dashed stubs.

Build order

The link wires go on the solder side, which inverts the usual perfboard order: wires last, after the parts, not before them.

  1. Make all 23 cuts, before any part goes inSpot-face cutter or a 3 mm drill twisted by hand, from the copper side. Meter every one across the gap — a partial cut is the hardest fault to find later.
  2. Fit the Pico on female headers, not soldered downTwo GND strips and the whole cut column run underneath it.
  3. Resistors, all six standing on endLying flat would put both leads on one strip and short the part out.
  4. LEDs — pad 1 is the cathode, and goes to row 27True for both D1 and D2. Check before soldering; they are the parts you cannot easily lift.
  5. Capacitors — C1 is polarised, C2 is notC1 is the 10 µF 25 V electrolytic across the 5 V rail at rows 3–4: its + lead (pad 1, the square pad) goes to row 3 and the striped − lead to the row-4 GND strip. Fitted backwards it can vent. C2 is a 100 nF ceramic across 3V3 at rows 24–26 and goes in either way round.
  6. J1 and J2J1 is a 2.50 mm connector on a 2.54 mm grid; the drift is 0.2 mm by pin 6 and the pins bend to suit.
  7. Link wires last, on the solder sideThey follow the parts rather than preceding them. Strip and tin both ends first.
  8. Meter before power-up: +5V–GND open, +3V3–GND openAnd U1.33 (AGND) isolated from GND — the specific thing the row-10 cut exists to guarantee.

Notes

Why the board is this size, and what is generated from what
  • The grid matches the stock the board is actually built on: a 40 × 40 hole Veroboard, 104.14 mm square, uncut. The circuit was laid out inside 28 × 30 and has not moved; the extra columns and rows are spare.
  • The board is generated, not hand-built. scripts/layout.py is pure data; the engine that turns it into copper lives in ~/KiCad/kicad-stripboard. Rebuild it, do not patch it.
  • This page is generated too, by scripts/wiring_guide.py, from the layout spec plus a read-back of the built board. Re-run it after any rebuild.
  • Renders are green FR4 rather than tan phenolic: the stackup API is not reachable from pcbnew here, so the Veroboard colour is a GUI job (Board Setup › Physical Stackup).
  • This is the Veroboard build. docs/perfboard-wiring.md covers a different, point-to-point board on plain perfboard. They are not interchangeable — pick one.
